# History of Tanauan

Tanauan, Leyte was **established in 1701** and has over 300 years of recorded history. It is known as the *"Cradle of Intellectuals"* (*Bungto Han Kamag-araman*) for producing many remarkable figures in Philippine history since the Spanish colonial period.

## The name "Tanauan"

The name comes from **"tan-awan"** (pronounced /tan-a-wan/), a *lookout tower*. The town derived its name from a majestic Molave tree that served as a natural lookout for early inhabitants. A sentinel watched from the tree for **Moro pirates** raiding coastal communities, and a hollow trunk called a *bayog* was hung from the tree and struck to signal danger, prompting residents to take refuge in a stone-walled fort called a *cuta*.

## Founding families

The first settlers were the family of **Calanao** (father) and **Makasanay** (mother) and their daughter **Sangad**, living by the banks of the Bukid River at the base of Adil Hill. In **1661**, **Juanillo Siengco**, a Chinese mestizo from Sugbo (Cebu), with his wife **Susana Baga** and companions Cornelio and Elias, journeyed to the eastern coast of Leyte along the Binahaan River and discovered fertile lands.

The two groups forged a pact of friendship and lived in harmony. In time, Juanillo's son **Josef** married Calanao's daughter **Sangad**; their five daughters and one son — Anurya, Alimocon, Atalabong, Dolido, and Tombalasay — formed the tribe that laid the groundwork for Tanauan's first town officials.

## World War II and Liberation

Following General Douglas MacArthur's landing on Red Beach in Palo on **October 20, 1944**, Tanauan became a crucial strategic site. The **US Sixth Army** established its headquarters here and a vital airfield (the [[Tanauan Airstrip]]) was constructed. Mayor **Pedro A. Villegas** and his secretary **Janario Perez** refused to serve Japanese forces and joined the guerrilla resistance. The 312th Bombardment Group also operated in the area.

## Resilience through disasters

From the **tidal wave of 1897** that the historic church withstood, to **Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan)** in November 2013, Tanauan has repeatedly demonstrated its capacity for recovery and rebuilding. The town motto: *"The spirit of the Tanauananon cannot be broken—only strengthened."*
