# Pasaka Festival

The **Pasaka Festival** is Tanauan's signature annual celebration. The name is derived from the **Waray word meaning "to invite,"** and honors the **Assumption of Our Lady** while commemorating Tanauan's cultural heritage. (Some heritage copy describes "Pasaka" as a term inviting progress, health, and good fortune.) It began in **1969** as a humble thanksgiving ritual and has grown into one of Eastern Visayas' most spectacular cultural events.

## When & where
- **Every August 1–15** in Tanauan, Leyte (peak near the town fiesta on Aug 15).
- **55+ years** of tradition.

## Festival highlights
- **Street dancing** — colorful costumes, synchronized choreography, rhythmic drumbeats
- **Religious procession** — solemn procession of the Señor Santo Niño
- **Community feast** — local delicacies and traditional Waray cuisine
- **Float parade** — floats representing different barangays telling stories of faith and heritage
- **Cultural exhibits** — Waray art, craftsmanship, historical artifacts
- **Live entertainment** — concerts, talent shows, local and national artists

## Timeline
- **1969:** beginnings honoring the Señor Santo Niño and the town's foundation anniversary
- **1980s:** regional recognition; more barangays joined with floats and synchronized dances
- **1990s:** street-dancing competitions formalized; performers from neighboring municipalities
- **2013:** after Typhoon Yolanda, Pasaka became a symbol of hope and resilience
- **Today:** one of the Philippines' most vibrant cultural celebrations

## Records
Grand Champion honors at the **Aliwan Fiesta and Pintados-Kasadyaan**. Recent festival archives show themes such as "Unity in Faith" (2024), "Rising Together" (2023), "Hope Renewed" (2022).
